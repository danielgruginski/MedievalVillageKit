# Medieval Village Kit

A procedural, hand-painted (World of Warcraft–style) medieval building and terrain kit made in Blender 4.4, for a Unity
colony sim. Everything — meshes, PBR textures, materials, the marching-squares terrain and the showcase maps — is
generated by the Python code in `src/`, which also lives inside the .blend as Blender texts.

- **Kit:** about 420 building pieces (walls, roofs, props, 8 themed modules, landmark smithy and inn, market stalls),
  39 nature pieces, 26 terrain tiles. Catalogue: [docs/PIECES.md](docs/PIECES.md).
- **Terrain:** dual-grid marching-squares tiles connected by their corners: cliffs, shores, ramps and stairs with cliff
  transitions, water, cobble paving with curbs.
- **Showcase:** a 216 × 168 m valley town (walls, gatehouse, river, lake, districts) plus a small terrain demo.
- **Interiors:** a modular interior kit for a top-down camera (146 pieces on a 1.5 m grid, walls in full and cut-away
  heights, furniture for homes, tavern, smithy, workshop and chapel) and nine showcase interiors, one scene per floor.
  Reference: [docs/INTERIOR_KIT.md](docs/INTERIOR_KIT.md).
- **Dungeons:** a dungeon kit on the same framework (46 pieces: dark masonry, iron bars and gates, light grates, ossuary
  niches, stone stairs, torches, cages, sarcophagi, treasure) and two linked levels, a gaol and a crypt.
  Reference: [docs/DUNGEON_KIT.md](docs/DUNGEON_KIT.md).
- **Adventuring places:** an adventure kit on top of it (331 pieces: traps and mechanisms, a sunken temple's ruins,
  organic caves on a painted cell map with winding chasms and pools, monster lairs, loot, transitions where masonry
  breaks into caves, a cave generator) and three more levels down the same descent: a trapped temple, caverns crossed
  by a rope bridge, a goblin warren. Reference: [docs/ADVENTURE_KIT.md](docs/ADVENTURE_KIT.md).
- **Sewers:** a sewer kit (30 pieces: brick tunnels, water channels as dual-grid tiles, culverts, an outfall, a
  sluice, bridges, a ladder up to the street, breaches into caves) and a town sewer level.
  Reference: [docs/SEWER_KIT.md](docs/SEWER_KIT.md).
- **Natural water:** a water kit (61 pieces: streams that wind through the cave maps, waterfalls from rock faces and
  over a chasm's lip, stepping stones, flow directions for Unity's water shaders) and an underground river level.
  Reference: [docs/WATER_KIT.md](docs/WATER_KIT.md).
- **Dwarven halls:** a dwarf kit (36 pieces: granite walls with a gilded rune band, corbelled doorways, a great gate
  with rune-carved stone leaves, lava channels with a hot core, pillars, a throne, king statues, braziers, a forge,
  red clan banners, a stone arch bridge, a gold floor medallion) and a great hall carved into the mountain, with a
  treasury and a foundry. Reference: [docs/DWARF_KIT.md](docs/DWARF_KIT.md).
- **Points of interest:** six landmark pieces, one per theme, each placed once in a level: a great wyrm's bones, a
  fallen colossus's head, a dwarven foundry's crucible, a necromancer's circle, the rat king's throne of junk, a sacred
  spring. Reference: [docs/POINTS_OF_INTEREST.md](docs/POINTS_OF_INTEREST.md).
- **Debris:** twenty pieces of loose debris (rocks, rubble, broken pottery, splintered wood, fallen masonry, lost
  gear, a cold campfire, ore, slag, rags) and a scatter pass that strews them over every adventure level by theme,
  gathering stone at the feet of walls and rock faces. Reference: [docs/DEBRIS.md](docs/DEBRIS.md).
- **Mines:** a mine kit (35 pieces: rail track laid from polylines with turntables at the junctions, carts, timber sets
  with lanterns, timber lining for the galleries, gold, iron and crystal veins, a timbered shaft with a great treadwheel
  over it, a timbered adit, ore piles, a sorting bench, a cave-in) and a mine under the dwarf hall, its galleries
  timber-lined, with its lift down and its adit up toward the town. Reference: [docs/MINE_KIT.md](docs/MINE_KIT.md).

## Quick start

1. Run `open_in_blender.bat` (Blender 4.4). The Blender MCP add-on starts its server on port 9876 by itself.
2. In Blender (Text Editor or MCP), load the kit and sync the code:

```python
import bpy
exec(open(r"E:\Unity\Projects\GameArtGeneration\MedievalVillageKit\tools\kit_sync.py", encoding="utf8").read())
status()                                   # texts vs src/ files; push("name") after editing a file
exec(bpy.data.texts["vk_kit"].as_string()); g = vk_kit_ns()
g["full_rebuild"]()                        # rebuild all kit pieces in place
exec(bpy.data.texts["vk_town_map"].as_string(), g); g["build_valley_town"]()   # rebuild the valley (60–90 s)
```

More commands (nature rebuild, terrain tests, renders): [docs/KIT_README.md §3](docs/KIT_README.md).

## Repository

Git tracks the code, tools, docs (with the gallery images), reviews, script logs and the small assets. The large
binaries stay on disk but are ignored (see `.gitignore`): the .blend (370+ MB, over GitHub's 100 MB limit),
`renders/` (~1.7 GB of work-in-progress renders), `assets/textures/` (generated, and packed inside the .blend) and
`assets/texture_dev/`. Back those up separately, or switch them to Git LFS (`git lfs track "*.blend"`) if a remote with
enough LFS storage is used.

The .blend is not published for a second reason: besides the kit it holds a `SpriteRig` scene with third-party
character assets that may not be redistributed. Strip that scene (and anything it references) before sharing the file.

Unity export is deliberately deferred until the Blender files are finished. Target project: `E:\Unity\Projects\MedievalSetting`.
